NUTRITION GUIDANCE

Healthy Diet Fundamentals

A healthy diet is based on adequacy, balance, moderation, and variety. The exact combination of foods should reflect individual needs, activity level, cultural context, food availability, and dietary preferences.

A varied diet can include vegetables, fruits, pulses, whole grains, nuts, seeds, dairy or alternatives, and appropriate animal-source foods.

Indian Meal Structure

A balanced Indian meal can combine a staple carbohydrate source, a protein-rich food, vegetables, and an appropriate amount of dietary fat.

Examples of staple foods include rice, roti, oats, millets, potatoes, and other grains or starchy foods. Protein sources can include dal, beans, chickpeas, soy foods, dairy, eggs, and other suitable foods.

Meals do not need to look identical every day. Variety across meals and across the week is useful.

Protein

Protein contributes to maintaining and building body tissues, including muscle.

Useful protein sources include eggs, milk, curd, paneer, soy foods, lentils, beans, chickpeas, peas, nuts, and seeds.

During fat loss, maintaining adequate protein intake alongside resistance training can support muscle retention. Individual protein requirements vary, so Arc should avoid presenting one protein target as universally appropriate.

Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates are an important source of energy. Whole grains, pulses, fruits, and vegetables can provide carbohydrates along with fibre and other nutrients.

Carbohydrates do not need to be eliminated for fat loss. The overall diet and energy balance matter more than removing one food group.

Dietary Fats

Dietary fat is an essential part of a healthy diet, but fats are energy-dense.

Unsaturated fats from foods such as nuts, seeds, and suitable vegetable oils can be useful choices. Saturated and industrial trans fats should generally be limited.

Portion Guidance

Portion needs vary between people and meals. A useful approach is to build meals around nutrient-dense foods and adjust portions according to hunger, activity, goals, and progress.

Portion guidance should not require weighing every ingredient. Simple visual or plate-based guidance can make healthy eating easier to maintain.

Fat Loss Nutrition

Fat loss generally requires sustained energy intake below energy expenditure over time.

A sustainable approach focuses on food quality, appropriate portions, adequate protein, regular meals when useful, and habits that can be maintained.

Aggressive restriction is not automatically better and can make adherence, recovery, and training more difficult.

Eggetarian Protein Sources

Eggetarian diets can include eggs and vegetarian protein sources such as milk, curd, paneer, tofu, soy, dal, rajma, chana, and other pulses.

Protein sources can be distributed across meals rather than relying on a single large serving.

Food Quality

Minimally processed foods can form the foundation of a healthy diet. Fruits, vegetables, pulses, whole grains, nuts, seeds, and suitable protein sources provide a broad range of nutrients.

Highly processed foods can still fit into a flexible diet, but foods high in free sugars, unhealthy fats, and sodium are generally better consumed less often.

Eating During Travel or Busy Days

Travel and busy schedules do not require abandoning healthy eating completely.

Useful strategies include choosing a protein source when available, adding vegetables or fruit, keeping portions reasonable, staying hydrated, and avoiding an all-or-nothing mindset.

One meal that differs from the usual routine does not undo long-term progress.
